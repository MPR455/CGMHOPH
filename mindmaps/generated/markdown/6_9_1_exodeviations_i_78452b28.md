# 6.9.1. Exodeviations (I)

## Hierarchy

- Introduction
  - manifest or latent divergent strabismus
  - frequency
    - varies among different ethnic groups
      - Europeans and North and South Americans
        - exodeviations:esodeviations = 1:3
      - Asians
        - exodeviations = esodeviations
  - risk factors
    - maternal smoking during pregnancy
    - premature birth
    - family history of strabismus
    - uncorrected refractive errors
      - similar to esodeviation
- Exophoria
  - exodeviation controlled by fusion under normal viewing conditions
    - detected when binocular vision is interrupted
      - alternate cover test
  - fairly common
  - symptoms
    - often asymptomatic if
      - angle of strabismus is small
      - fusional convergence amplitudes are adequate
    - prolonged, detailed visual work may bring about asthenopia
  - breakdown of an exophoria to an exotropia may occur transiently
    - during a serious illness
    - after ingestion of sedatives or alcohol
  - treatment
    - usually not necessary unless
      - exophoria progresses to intermittent exotropia
      - causes asthenopic symptoms
- Pseudoexotropia
  - appearance of exodeviation when in fact the eyes are properly aligned
  - less common than pseudoesotropia
  - etiology
    - wide interpupillary distance
    - positive angle kappa without other ocular abnormalities
    - positive angle kappa together with ocular abnormalities such as temporal dragging of the macula in retinopathy of prematurity
