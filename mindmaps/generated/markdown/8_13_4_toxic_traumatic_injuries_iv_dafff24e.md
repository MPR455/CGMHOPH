# 8.13.4. Toxic & Traumatic Injuries (IV)

## Hierarchy

- Insect Injuries
  - bee and wasp stings to the cornea and/or conjunctiva
    - acute response
      - conjunctival hyperemia and chemosis
      - pain
      - corneal edema
      - corneal infiltration
        - decreased vision
      - rare sequelae
        - hyphema
        - lenticular opacities
        - iritis
        - secondary glaucoma
        - heterochromia
    - treatment
      - cycloplegics
      - topical & occasionally systemic corticosteroids
      - removal of externalized stingers
        - retained stingers may remain inert in the cornea for years
  - caterpillar and tarantula hairs
    - become embedded in the cornea and conjunctiva
    - very fine
      - extreme foreign-body sensation
        - until the hairs migrate below the corneal surface
      - usually cannot be removed manually
    - tend to migrate more deeply into ocular tissues
      - localized granulomatous inflammatory response (ophthalmia nodosum)
    - treatment
      - topical corticosteroids
- Vegetation Injuries
  - plant sources are common causes of fungal keratitis
  - sap (latex)
    - acute keratoconjunctivitis
    - epithelial defect
    - stromal infiltration
    - pencil tree
      - tropical regions
    - manchineel tree
      - tropical regions
    - dieffenbachia
      - household plant
      - deposition of calcium oxalate crystals in the cornea
  - corneal foreign bodies
    - coconut shell
    - sunflower stalk
    - ornamental cactus
  - treatment
    - irrigation
    - removal of foreign bodies
    - topical cycloplegics
    - prophylactic antibiotic coverage
    - corticosteroids are best avoided
      - may promote fungal infection
    - surgical removal of foreign bodies
      - uncontrolled inflammatory response
      - secondary microbial infections
