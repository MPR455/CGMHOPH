# 5.14.8. Systemic Conditions (VIII): Neuro-Ophthalmic Disorders Associated With Pregnancy

## Hierarchy

- Introduction
  - conditions occurring with greater frequency/ exacerbated during pregnancy (or postpartum period)
    - cerebral venous thrombosis
    - posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES)
    - pituitary macroadenomas
      - pituitary apoplexy
      - lymphocytic hypophysitis
  - tumors that undergo expansion during pregnancy
    - meningiomas
    - orbital hemangiomas
    - choroidal hemangiomas
- Lymphocytic Hypophysitis
  - rare
  - autoimmune inflammation of the pituitary gland
    - lymphocytic infiltrate
      - can heal with minimal sequelae or progress to fibrosis
  - clinical presentation
    - various degrees of pituitary dysfunction
      - ± permanent hypopituitarism
    - acute-onset diabetes insipidus
    - chiasmal visual field defects
    - may mimic a pituitary tumor
    - diagnosis should be considered if these symptoms and signs occur during pregnancy
  - imaging/endocrine testing are not specific
  - surgical treatment if vision is compromised
- Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome
  - clinical presentation
    - headache
    - altered mental status
    - seizures
    - visual disturbances
      - transient visual loss
      - scotomata
      - photopsias
      - dimming of vision
  - MRI
    - edema involving the white matter of the cerebral posterior regions
      - especially parieto-occipital lobes
      - ± frontal and temporal lobes
  - etiology
    - acute hypertension
    - preeclampsia/eclampsia
    - immunosuppressive drugs
      - cyclosporine
      - tacrolimus
  - prognosis
    - abnormalities visible on MRI are reversible
    - visual prognosis is usually excellent
