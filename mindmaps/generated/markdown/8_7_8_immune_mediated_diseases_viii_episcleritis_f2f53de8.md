# 8.7.8. Immune-Mediated Diseases (VIII): Episcleritis

## Hierarchy

- Pathogenesis
  - only a minority have underlying systemic disease
    - autoimmune connective tissue disease
      - sjögren syndrome
      - rheumatoid arthritis
    - gout
    - herpes zoster
    - syphilis
    - tuberculosis
    - lyme disease
    - rosacea
- Management
  - systemic workup
    - rarely indicated
    - autoimmune connective tissue disease
      - except after multiple recurrences
  - topical or oral NSAIDS
    - for patients bothered by pain
  - lubricants
  - topical steroid
    - should be kept to a minimum
      - cases that do not respond to lubricants and NSAIDS
- Clinical Presentation
  - 20–50 years
  - F>M
  - sudden-onset
  - bilateral disease
    - 1/3
      - deep pain of scleritis is worse than the pain associated with episcleritis
  - irritation or pain
  - slight tenderness
  - types
    - simple (diffuse injection)
      - localized to a sector of the globe
        - 70%
      - entire episclera
        - 30%
    - nodular
      - localized mobile nodule
  - redness
    - bright red or salmon pink
    - exposed interpalpebral zone
      - area of a pinguecula
        - scleritis has a violaceous hue
    - inflammation is superficial
      - will blanch with application of 2.5% topical phenylephrine
        - unlike scleritis
      - no scleral edema!
  - small peripheral corneal opacities
    - adjacent to the area of episcleral inflammation
    - 10%
  - transient
    - days to weeks
  - may recur
    - same or different locations
      - resolves without lasting destructive effects
