# 9.5.4. Clinical approach to uveitis (III): Differential Diagnosis & Epidemiology

## Hierarchy

- Epidemiology of Uveitis
  - 10% of all blindness cases in the US
    - blindness attributable to uveitis
      - 15 new cases/100,000 persons/year
  - epidemiologic study of uveitis in northern California
    - incidence of 52.4/100,000 person-years
      - 3x higher than previously reported in studies from the United States
    - incidence/prevalence lowest in pediatric age group
      - 30/100,000
    - incidence/prevalence highest >65 years
      - 151.3/100,000
  - most cases are chronic and bilateral
    - females are slightly more commonly affected
    - carry significant complication rates
  - anterior involvement>panuveitis>posterior uveitis>intermediate uveitis
  - See Table 5-11
    - university/referral-based studies overestimate the prevalence of intermediate and posterior uveitis
- Differential Diagnosis of Uveitic Entities
  - infectious
    - viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and helminths
  - noninfectious
    - immunologic/allergic origin
    - masquerade syndromes
      - intraocular lymphoma
      - retinoblastoma
      - leukemia
      - malignant melanoma
    - idiopathic
    - others
      - juvenile xanthogranuloma
      - pigment dispersion syndrome
      - retinal detachment
      - retinitis pigmentosa
      - ocular ischemia syndrome
  - pattern recognition
    - frequently sufficient to establish a definitive diagnosis
    - naming-meshing system
      - the most likely causes are ranked based on how well the individual patient’s uveitis “fits” with the various known uveitic entities
    - many patients do not present with the classic signs and symptoms of a particular disease
      - require ongoing monitoring
    - presentation of a disease can also be modified by previous therapy or by a delay in evaluation
