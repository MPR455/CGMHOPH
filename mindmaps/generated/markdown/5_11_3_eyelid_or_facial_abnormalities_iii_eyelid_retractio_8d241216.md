# 5.11.3. Eyelid or Facial Abnormalities (III): Eyelid Retraction

## Hierarchy

- Causes
  - Mechanical
  - Myogenic
  - Neurogenic
  - See Table 11-2
- bilateral eyelid retraction
  - thyroid eye disease
  - familial periodic paralysis
  - Cushing syndrome
  - midbrain disease
  - hydrocephalus with vertical nystagmus
- preterm infants
  - with the eyes in primary position, the sclera is visible above the superior corneal limbus
  - ± benign transient conjugate downgaze associated with upper eyelid retraction
- normal infants
  - 80% of children 14–18 weeks of age
  - eye-popping reflex when ambient lighting levels are reduced
- unilateral eyelid retraction
  - thyroid eye disease
  - aberrant regeneration of the third nerve
  - Marcus Gunn jaw-winking syndrome
  - idiopathic levator fibrosis
  - carcinomatous infiltration
- thyroid eye disease
  - most common cause
- dorsal midbrain syndrome
  - Collier sign
- treatment
  - subconjunctival or transcutaneous injections of botulinum toxin
  - several surgical procedures
  - in patients with thyroid eye disease
- unilateral eyelid retraction as a result of contralateral ptosis
  - patients with levator aponeurotic defects
  - Hering’s law of equal innervation
