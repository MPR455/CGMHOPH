# 9.7.23. Infectious Ocular Inflammatory Diseases (XXIII): Infectious Scleritis

## Hierarchy

- organisms
  - Pseudomonas
    - most common after pterygium excision
  - Actinomyces
  - Nocardia
  - mycobacteria
  - fungi
    - Fusarium and Aspergillus species
  - gram-positive cocci
    - Staphylococcus pneumococcus and Streptococcus
  - HSV and VZV
- diagnostic Workup
  - culture
    - base and edges
  - lamellar scleral biopsy
    - if the condition worsens on anti-inflammatory therapy alone
  - diagnostic evaluation for noninfectious necrotizing scleritis
- treatment
  - debridement of infected tissues
  - antibiotics
    - topically (including by drop or continuous lavage)
    - subconjunctivally
    - systemically
  - corticosteroids
  - scleral patch grafting
    - avoided initially
    - if there is severe thinning
- predisposing conditions
  - previous ocular surgery
    - pterygium surgery
      - especially when beta irradiation or mitomycin C is utilized
    - scleral buckling
    - cataract surgery
    - pars plana vitrectomy
    - precipitating surgery may be recent or remote
  - penetrating injury contaminated by soil or vegetable matter
- prognosis
  - difficult to treat
    - inadequate excision of infected tissues can result in disease recurrence
    - long-term treatment may be necessary
  - complications are common
    - +- globe perforation with surgery
    - extension of the infection to the cornea
    - endophthalmitis
      - marginal corneal infiltrates
- clinical presentation
  - symptoms
    - pain
    - redness
    - decreased vision
  - sclera appears necrotic, thin, and avascular
    - inflammation at the edges
    - at the site of a surgical or traumatic wound
  - +- mucopurulent discharge
